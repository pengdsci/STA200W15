Question 1

Which statement best describes the shape of a Chi-square (χ2) distribution as the degrees of freedom increase?

  1. It becomes more skewed to the right.
  2. It becomes more symmetric.
  3. It becomes perfectly normal.
  4. It becomes skewed to the left.

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note states that the χ2 distribution is “Skewed right, but becomes more symmetric as degrees of freedom (df) increase.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The skewness decreases, not increases, with larger df.
  • C: It never becomes exactly normal, though it approaches symmetry.
  • D: The χ2 distribution is always non-negative and right-skewed; it never becomes left-skewed.
Question 2

What is the support (set of possible values) of a Chi-square random variable?

  1. All real numbers.
  2. Only positive integers.
  3. Non-negative real numbers (x ≥ 0).
  4. Only values between 0 and 1.

Correct answer: C

Justification: The note states: “Support: Defined only for non-negative values (x ≥ 0).”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The χ2 distribution does not take negative values.
  • B: It is continuous, so it can take any non-negative real value, not just integers.
  • D: The range is not restricted to [0, 1]; it extends to infinity.
Question 3

Which parameter fully determines a Chi-square distribution?

  1. The mean.
  2. The variance.
  3. The degrees of freedom (df).
  4. The sample size.

Correct answer: C

Justification: The note states: “Parameter: Degrees of freedom (df), typically a positive integer.” Both the mean (= df) and the variance (= 2·df) are derived from df, so df alone determines the distribution.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A and B: Mean and variance are derived from df, not independent parameters.
  • D: Sample size is not a parameter of the χ2 distribution itself.
Question 4

In a goodness-of-fit test for a categorical variable with k categories, what are the degrees of freedom for the Chi-square test statistic?

  1. k
  2. k − 1
  3. n − 1 (where n is the sample size)
  4. n − k

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note states: “The degrees of freedom are always (k − 1) if the categorical factor variable has k levels.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: Using k would overstate the df; one df is lost because the probabilities must sum to 1.
  • C: n − 1 is the df for a one-sample test of a proportion, not for a multi-category goodness-of-fit test.
  • D: n − k is not used in this context.
Question 5

Why is the Chi-square goodness-of-fit test always a right-tailed test?

  1. Because the χ2 distribution is symmetric.
  2. Because the test statistic measures discrepancy, and large values indicate lack of fit.
  3. Because the alternative hypothesis is always “less than.”
  4. Because the p-value is always the left-tail area.

Correct answer: B

Justification: “All χ2 tests are right-tailed since the test statistic measures the discrepancy between two distributions.” A small G2 indicates lack of evidence against H0; a large G2 falls in the right tail.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The χ2 distribution is skewed, not symmetric.
  • C: The alternative is typically “not equal” or “the distribution is different,” not “less than.”
  • D: The p-value is the right-tail area, not the left-tail area.
Question 6

The R command pchisq(5, df = 10) returns

> pchisq(5, df = 10)
## [1] 0.108822

What probability does this represent?

  1. P(X ≥ 5) for a χ2 distribution with 10 df.
  2. P(X ≤ 5) for a χ2 distribution with 10 df.
  3. P(X = 5) for a χ2 distribution with 10 df.
  4. The 5th percentile of a χ2 distribution with 10 df.

Correct answer: B

Justification: By default pchisq(x, df) returns the lower-tail probability P(X ≤ x). The note explicitly shows this example: pchisq(5, df = 10) gives 0.108822.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The upper-tail probability would require lower.tail = FALSE.
  • C: The χ2 distribution is continuous, so the probability of an exact value is 0.
  • D: A percentile would be obtained with qchisq(), not pchisq().
Question 7

To compute P(X ≥ 15) for a χ2 random variable with 10 degrees of freedom, which R command is correct?

  1. pchisq(15, df = 10)
  2. pchisq(15, df = 10, lower.tail = FALSE)
  3. qchisq(15, df = 10)
  4. pchisq(15, df = 10, lower.tail = TRUE)

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note states that pchisq(x, df, lower.tail = FALSE) returns the upper-tail area P(X ≥ x). Example 2 shows pchisq(15, df = 10, lower.tail = FALSE) giving 0.1320619.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A and D: lower.tail = TRUE (the default) gives P(X ≤ 15), not P(X ≥ 15).
  • C: qchisq() returns quantiles, not probabilities.
Question 8

In the fair-die example, the die is rolled 60 times. Under the null hypothesis that the die is fair, what is the expected frequency for each face?

  1. 6
  2. 10
  3. 12
  4. 60

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note states: “Under the null hypothesis, we have expected frequencies of the 6 categories to be equal to 10” because 60 × (1/6) = 10.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: 6 is the number of faces, not the expected count.
  • C: 12 is one of the observed frequencies, not the expected.
  • D: 60 is the total number of rolls.
Question 9

Using the fair-die example (60 rolls, expected frequency 10 for each face), the observed frequencies are 12, 7, 14, 15, 4, 8. What is the value of the Chi-square test statistic G2?

  1. 5.0
  2. 9.4
  3. 12.0
  4. 15.0

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note computes

G2 = (12−10)2/10 + (7−10)2/10 + (14−10)2/10 + (15−10)2/10 + (4−10)2/10 + (8−10)2/10
= 0.4 + 0.9 + 1.6 + 2.5 + 3.6 + 0.4 = 9.4.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A, C, D: These are not the result of the correct calculation.
Question 10

For a Chi-square distribution with 5 degrees of freedom, what is the critical value at the 0.05 significance level?

  1. 1.145
  2. 11.071
  3. 15.086
  4. 3.841

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note explicitly gives CV = χ25, 0.05 = 11.071.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: 1.145 is not the 5% critical value for df = 5 (the 5% critical value for df = 1 is 3.841).
  • C: 15.086 is the critical value for df = 5 at α = 0.01.
  • D: 3.841 is the critical value for df = 1 at α = 0.05.
Question 11

In the grade-distribution example (five categories: A, B, C, D, F), what are the degrees of freedom for the Chi-square goodness-of-fit test?

  1. 5
  2. 4
  3. 99
  4. 100

Correct answer: B

Justification: With k = 5 categories, df = k − 1 = 4.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: Using 5 overstates the df by one, because the cell probabilities must sum to 1 (one constraint).
  • C and D: These involve the sample size, which is not used for df in this test.
Question 12

What does the null hypothesis in a Chi-square goodness-of-fit test specify?

  1. The population mean.
  2. The cell probabilities (the hypothesised distribution).
  3. The sample frequencies.
  4. The degrees of freedom.

Correct answer: B

Justification: “For a single categorical variable, the null hypothesis should specify the cell probabilities… (p1, p2, …, pk) must be specified in the null hypothesis.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The test concerns categorical distributions, not means.
  • C: Sample frequencies are the observed data, not the null hypothesis.
  • D: Degrees of freedom are a property of the test, not the hypothesis itself.
Question 13

A Chi-square goodness-of-fit test yields G2 = 2.97 with 5 df and a p-value of 0.705. What does a small G2 indicate?

  1. Strong evidence against H0.
  2. Little evidence against H0 (fail to reject H0).
  3. The expected frequencies are too small.
  4. The test is invalid.

Correct answer: B

Justification: “A small G2 indicates a lack of evidence for rejecting the null hypothesis.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: Large G2 values provide evidence against H0; small values do not.
  • C: A small G2 does not imply small expected frequencies.
  • D: A small G2 does not invalidate the test.

Which R function performs a Chi-square goodness-of-fit test directly?

  4. chisq.goftest()

Question 15

When using chisq.test() for a goodness-of-fit test, what must the p argument contain?

Question 16

The R output below comes from a goodness-of-fit test:

## Chi-squared test for given probabilities
##
## data:  observed
## X-squared = 2.9667, df = 5, p-value = 0.7051

At α = 0.05, what is the correct conclusion?

  1. Reject H0; the distribution is significantly different.
  2. Fail to reject H0; there is not enough evidence to say the distribution differs.
  3. Accept H0; the distribution is exactly as hypothesised.
  4. The test is inconclusive because the p-value is too large.

Correct answer: B

Justification: Because the p-value (0.7051) is greater than 0.05, we do not reject H0. The note states: “Since the p-value is greater than 0.05, the null hypothesis is not rejected.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: Rejection would require p < 0.05.
  • C: We never “accept” H0; we only fail to reject it.
  • D: A large p-value is not inconclusive; it simply indicates insufficient evidence against H0.
Question 17

In a right-tailed Chi-square test, what does the p-value represent?

  1. The area to the left of the test statistic.
  2. The area to the right of the test statistic.
  3. The probability that H0 is true.
  4. The probability of a Type I error.

Correct answer: B

Justification: “The p-value of a χ2 test is always the area of the tail region to the right-hand side of the test statistic.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The left-tail area is not the p-value for a right-tailed test.
  • C: The p-value is not the probability that H0 is true.
  • D: The Type I error rate is α, not the p-value.
Question 18

How are the expected frequencies computed under the null hypothesis in a goodness-of-fit test?

  1. By taking the average of the observed frequencies.
  2. By multiplying the total sample size by the hypothesised probabilities.
  3. By using the observed proportions.
  4. By dividing the total sample size by the number of categories.

Correct answer: B

Justification: The note shows in R: expected.counts <- tot * expected.props, i.e., total count times hypothesised probabilities.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: The average of observed frequencies ignores the hypothesised probabilities.
  • C: Observed proportions are not the null hypothesis.
  • D: Dividing by the number of categories assumes equal probabilities, which is not always the case.
Question 19

What is the general formula for the Chi-square goodness-of-fit test statistic?

  1. Σ(Oi − Ei)2
  2. Σ|Oi − Ei|
  3. Σ(Oi − Ei)2 / Ei
  4. Σ(Oi / Ei)

Correct answer: C

Justification: The note defines

G2 = (O1 − E1)2/E1 + (O2 − E2)2/E2 + … + (Ok − Ek)2/Ek.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A: Omits the division by Ei.
  • B: Uses absolute differences instead of squared differences.
  • D: Is not the χ2 statistic.
Question 20

In the motivational example about STEM majors (H0: p = 0.30, n = 100, observed STEM = 33, non-STEM = 67), what is the value of the Chi-square test statistic?

  1. 0.43
  2. 0.86
  3. 1.00
  4. 2.00

Correct answer: A

Justification: Under H0, the expected counts are ESTEM = 100 × 0.30 = 30 and Enon-STEM = 100 × 0.70 = 70. Therefore

TS = (33 − 30)2/30 + (67 − 70)2/70 = 9/30 + 9/70 = 0.300 + 0.129 ≈ 0.43.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B: 0.86 is about twice the correct value and does not follow from the data.
  • C: 1.00 comes from the arithmetic slip (67 − 70)2 = 49 (instead of 9), which would give 9/30 + 49/70 = 0.3 + 0.7 = 1.0. But (67 − 70)2 = (−3)2 = 9.
  • D: 2.00 has no basis in these data.
