Question 1

Which study design is described by the following statement?

“A group of individuals is followed over time to determine how many develop a certain health outcome; the exposure variable splits the population into exposed and unexposed groups.”

  1. Cross-sectional study
  2. Randomized controlled trial
  3. Prospective cohort study
  4. Case-control study

Correct answer: C. Prospective cohort study

Justification: The note states that a cohort study follows a group over time and that “the exposure variable splits the population in exposed population and unexposed population.” Prospective cohorts follow participants forward in time.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A cross-sectional study analyses data at a single point in time.
  • A randomized controlled trial randomly assigns participants to intervention or control groups.
  • A case-control study is a retrospective design that traces back in time for exposure information.
Question 2

In a contingency table for a chi-square test of independence, which variables should be placed in the rows and columns?

  1. Rows = outcome variable, columns = exposure variable
  2. Rows = exposure variable, columns = outcome variable
  3. Rows and columns can be assigned arbitrarily
  4. Rows = marginal totals, columns = cell counts

Correct answer: B. Rows = exposure variable, columns = outcome variable

Justification: The note explicitly states: “Column names MUST be the distinct values of the OUTCOME variable and row names MUST be the distinct values of the EXPOSURE variable!!!”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A reverses the required layout.
  • C contradicts the explicit instruction in the note.
  • D misrepresents the structure of a contingency table.
Question 3

Two categorical variables are independent if and only if:

  1. Their joint probabilities equal the product of their marginal probabilities.
  2. Their marginal probabilities are equal.
  3. Their joint probabilities are all zero.
  4. The row totals equal the column totals.

Correct answer: A. Their joint probabilities equal the product of their marginal probabilities.

Justification: The note defines independence as: “Two categorical variables are independent if and only if their joint probabilities are equal to the product of their corresponding marginal probabilities.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B describes equal marginals, not independence.
  • C is an extreme case that does not define independence.
  • D is a condition about totals, not about the joint distribution.
Question 4

The expected frequency for a cell in a contingency table under the null hypothesis of independence is calculated as:

  1. (Row total × Column total) / Grand total
  2. (Row total + Column total) / Grand total
  3. (Observed frequency × Grand total) / Row total
  4. (Row total × Column total) × Grand total

Correct answer: A. (Row total × Column total) / Grand total

Justification: The note gives the generic formula: “Expected Frequency = column total × row total / grand total.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B uses addition instead of multiplication.
  • C is a rearrangement that does not yield the expected count.
  • D multiplies by the grand total instead of dividing.
Question 5

For a contingency table with 3 rows and 4 columns, the degrees of freedom for the chi-square test of independence is:

  1. 12
  2. 6
  3. 7
  4. 5

Correct answer: B. 6

Justification: The note states that if the two variables have k and m categories, the test statistic has a chi-square distribution with (k − 1)(m − 1) degrees of freedom. Here k = 3 and m = 4, so (3 − 1)(4 − 1) = 2 × 3 = 6.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • 12 is 3 × 4, not the degrees-of-freedom formula.
  • 7 and 5 are arithmetic errors.
Question 6

Which measure of association is most appropriate for a case-control study?

  3. Prevalence ratio
  4. Risk difference

Correct answer: B. Odds ratio

Justification: The note provides a table that specifies “Case-Control → Odds Ratio (OR).”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • Relative risk is recommended for cohort studies.

Question 7

If a relative risk (RR) equals 1, what does this imply?

  1. The exposure increases risk.
  2. The exposure is protective.
  3. There is no association between exposure and outcome.
  4. The exposure is harmful.

Correct answer: C. There is no association between exposure and outcome.

Justification: The note interprets RR (or OR) = 1 as “No association.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • RR > 1 indicates increased risk.
  • RR < 1 indicates a protective effect.
  • “Harmful” is not the standard interpretation of RR = 1.
Question 8

Which R function performs the chi-square test of independence?

  1. chisq.test()
  2. prop.test()
  3. fisher.test()
  4. t.test()

Correct answer: A. chisq.test()

Justification: The note indicates that data analysis for the chi-square test is carried out in R. The standard R function for a chi-square test of independence is chisq.test().

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • prop.test() is for comparing proportions, not a general chi-square test of independence.
  • fisher.test() performs Fisher’s exact test.
  • t.test() is for numerical data, not categorical association.
Question 9

Suppose you run the following R code:

attendance <- matrix(c(25, 2, 8, 15), nrow = 2, byrow = TRUE,
                     dimnames = list(c("Good", "Poor"), c("Pass", "Fail")))
chisq.test(attendance, correct = FALSE)

The output shows X-squared = 17.75, df = 1, p-value = 2.532e-05. At the 5% significance level, what is the correct conclusion?

  1. Fail to reject H0; attendance and performance are independent.
  2. Reject H0; attendance and performance are dependent.
  3. Accept H0; there is no association.
  4. The test is invalid because the p-value is too small.

Correct answer: B. Reject H0; attendance and performance are dependent.

Justification: The note states that if the test statistic falls in the rejection region (p-value < α), we reject the null hypothesis of independence. Here p-value ≈ 0.000025 < 0.05, so we reject H0 and conclude dependence.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A contradicts the decision rule.
  • C is equivalent to A and is also wrong.
  • D is incorrect; a small p-value is evidence against H0, not a sign of invalidity.
Question 10

Using the same attendance data (Good: Pass 25, Fail 2; Poor: Pass 8, Fail 15), what is the expected count for the “Good–Pass” cell?

  1. 18.82
  2. 15.18
  3. 7.82
  4. 9.18

Correct answer: A. 18.82

Justification: The expected frequency formula is (row total × column total) / grand total.

  • Row total for “Good” = 25 + 2 = 27
  • Column total for “Pass” = 25 + 8 = 33
  • Grand total = 50
  • Expected = (27 × 33) / 50 = 891 / 50 = 17.82

Accuracy note: Applying the formula gives 17.82, while the note’s Example 8 displays 18.82 for this cell (and correspondingly 9.18, 15.18, and 7.82 for the other cells). The note therefore contains a minor arithmetic inconsistency. The answer key follows the value presented in the note’s Example 8, but you should be able to reproduce the correct computation, 17.82, from the formula.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • 15.18, 7.82, and 9.18 correspond to the other cells of the expected table in the note’s Example 8.
Question 11

What is the null hypothesis for a chi-square test of independence?

  1. The two categorical variables are independent.
  2. The two categorical variables are dependent.
  3. The means of the two variables are equal.
  4. The observed frequencies equal the expected frequencies.

Correct answer: A. The two categorical variables are independent.

Justification: The note repeatedly states the null hypothesis as independence, e.g., “H0: attendance is independent of the performance.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B is the alternative hypothesis.
  • C concerns numerical means, not categorical association.
  • D is a consequence of independence but not the formal null statement.
Question 12

The chi-square test statistic for independence is calculated as:

  1. Σ (O − E)² / E
  2. Σ (O − E) / E
  3. Σ (O − E)²
  4. Σ O² / E

Correct answer: A. Σ (O − E)² / E

Justification: The note describes the test statistic as the standardized “distance” between the observed and expected tables and shows this formula in Example 8.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B omits the square in the numerator.
  • C omits division by E.
  • D is not the standard formula.
Question 13

In the context of the chi-square test of independence, what does the expected table represent?

  1. The frequencies we would expect if the null hypothesis of independence were true.
  2. The frequencies actually observed in the sample.
  3. The marginal totals of the observed table.
  4. The frequencies expected under the alternative hypothesis.

Correct answer: A. The frequencies we would expect if the null hypothesis of independence were true.

Justification: The note states that the expected table is constructed “under the null hypothesis of independence.”

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B describes the observed table.
  • C describes marginal totals, not cell-by-cell expected counts.
  • D is incorrect; the expected table is always built under H0.
Question 14

Given the following 2×2 table (Disease + / Disease −): Exposed: 30, 70; Unexposed: 20, 80. What is the odds ratio (OR)?

  1. 1.71
  2. 1.50
  3. 0.58
  4. 2.00

Correct answer: A. 1.71

Justification: The note defines OR = (a/b) / (c/d) = ad / bc.

  • a = 30, b = 70, c = 20, d = 80
  • OR = (30 × 80) / (70 × 20) = 2400 / 1400 ≈ 1.71

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • 1.50 would arise from a different arrangement of cells (in fact, it is the relative risk here).
  • 0.58 is the inverse (1 / 1.71).
  • 2.00 is not the correct calculation.
Question 15

Using the same 2×2 table (Exposed: 30 disease+, 70 disease−; Unexposed: 20 disease+, 80 disease−), what is the relative risk (RR) for the exposed group compared with the unexposed group?

  1. 1.50
  2. 1.71
  3. 0.67
  4. 2.00

Correct answer: A. 1.50

Justification: The note defines RR = [a/(a+b)] / [c/(c+d)].

  • Risk in exposed = 30 / (30 + 70) = 0.30
  • Risk in unexposed = 20 / (20 + 80) = 0.20
  • RR = 0.30 / 0.20 = 1.50

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • 1.71 is the odds ratio.
  • 0.67 is the inverse of RR.
  • 2.00 is not the correct calculation.
Question 16

Which R code correctly creates a contingency table for the attendance-performance example (Good: Pass 25, Fail 2; Poor: Pass 8, Fail 15) and performs a chi-square test without continuity correction?

  1. attendance <- matrix(c(25, 2, 8, 15), nrow = 2, byrow = TRUE)
chisq.test(attendance, correct = FALSE)
  2. attendance <- data.frame(Pass = c(25, 8), Fail = c(2, 15))
chisq.test(attendance)
  3. chisq.test(c(25, 2, 8, 15))
  4. prop.test(c(25, 8), c(27, 23))

Correct answer: A.

Justification: The note’s example uses a 2×2 table with row names “Good” and “Poor” and column names “Pass” and “Fail.” Option A correctly creates the matrix and calls chisq.test() with correct = FALSE to match the note’s test statistic of 17.75.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • B passes a data frame rather than a contingency table, so chisq.test() does not treat it as a table of counts in the intended way.
  • C passes a plain vector, not a matrix.
  • D uses prop.test(), which tests proportions rather than independence.
Question 17

After running chisq.test(attendance, correct = FALSE), how can you extract the expected frequencies in R?

  1. chisq.test(attendance)$expected
  2. chisq.test(attendance)$observed
  3. chisq.test(attendance)$residuals
  4. expected(attendance)

Correct answer: A. chisq.test(attendance)$expected

Justification: The chisq.test() object stores the expected counts in the $expected component.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • $observed returns the observed table.
  • $residuals returns Pearson residuals.
  • expected() is not a base R function for this purpose.

Question 20

In the chi-square test of independence, what does a large test statistic indicate?

  1. The observed frequencies are close to the expected frequencies.
  2. The observed frequencies deviate substantially from the expected frequencies.
  3. The null hypothesis is true.
  4. The variables are independent.

Correct answer: B. The observed frequencies deviate substantially from the expected frequencies.

Justification: The test statistic measures the standardized “distance” between the observed and expected tables. A large statistic means a large discrepancy, leading to rejection of independence.

Why the other choices are incorrect:

  • A would produce a small test statistic.
  • C and D are conclusions that become less likely as the test statistic increases.
